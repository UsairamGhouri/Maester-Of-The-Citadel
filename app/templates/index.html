The Maester's Rulebook

Welcome to the Grand Archives. To extract the most accurate knowledge from the Citadel, you must follow these tenets of inquiry:

1. Frame Complete Questions

The Maester cannot read minds. Do not use isolated keywords like "chomsky and interrupts" or "CFG". Instead, ask complete, direct questions: "What is Chomsky's hierarchy, and how does it relate to interrupts?" or "Explain the definition of a Context-Free Grammar."

2. Be Specific with Terminology

The Citadel retrieves knowledge based on exact semantic meaning. Use the precise vocabulary that appears in your uploaded tomes. If a term has multiple meanings, provide context (e.g., "In the context of x86 architecture, what is a hardware interrupt?").

3. Limit the Scope

Do not ask the Maester to summarize an entire book in one breath. Focus on specific definitions, theorems, mechanisms, or historical facts to ensure the retrieved scrolls contain the exact answers you seek. (e.g., instead of "Summarize everything about parsing", ask "What are the steps involved in top-down parsing?").

4. The Law of Strict Adherence

The Maester is strictly forbidden from using pre-trained outside knowledge. If the answer to your question is not contained within the uploaded tomes, he will refuse to answer. This guarantees 100% factual accuracy based solely on your library. (e.g., asking "What is a GPU?" when the uploaded book only covers CPUs will result in a refusal, rather than an hallucinated answer).

- The Grand Maester
